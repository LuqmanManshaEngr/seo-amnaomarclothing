## 1. Clicks Vs. Impressions Trend

![Clicks vs Impressions](images/AOC_Clicks_and_Impressions_trend.png)<img width="2180" height="1169" alt="AOC- Clicks and Impressions trend" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/99a124d1-d868-4832-ba05-2efb18b027f5" />

- Impressions grew from **6 in March to a peak of 230 in September** and stayed high in October (208).
- Clicks peaked twice: **13 in April** (when only 37 impressions existed) and **18 in September**.
- Between June and August, impressions held steady (71–105) while clicks fell to 3, the weakest stretch of the campaign.
- **November (11 days)** shows 82 impressions, about 7.5 per day — in line with September (7.7/day) and October (6.7/day), so the daily pace has held.

---

## 2. CTR Vs. Average Position

![CTR vs Position](images/AOC_CTR_and_Average_Position_trend.png)<img width="2180" height="1169" alt="AOC- CTR and Average Position trend" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/16029826-1305-4d40-a028-d203aeca4086" />

- CTR peaked at **35.1% in April** on tiny volume, then settled into a 3–8% band as impressions grew. That is expected: early impressions come almost entirely from people searching the brand name, which clicks well.
- Average position drifted from 4.3 (April) to a low of **10.2 in August**, then recovered to 8.0 (September) and 9.2 (October). November's 6.5 rests on only 82 impressions.
- Position is impression-weighted, so months with more impressions (including non-brand queries ranking lower) naturally average worse. A wider net lowers the average even when the pages that matter hold steady.

## 3. Device Breakdown

![Device Breakdown](images/AOC_Device_breakdown.png)<img width="2114" height="1277" alt="AOC- Device breakdown" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8d3b0807-8b9e-46bd-a2f9-761689120f85" />

- **Mobile delivered 60 of 70 clicks (86%)** with a 10.0% CTR and an average position of 5.6.
- Desktop earned 300 impressions but only 10 clicks (3.3% CTR) at an average position of 12.9 — it is ranking much lower there.
- Priority: keep mobile experience and Core Web Vitals first, and investigate why desktop positions are so much weaker.

## 5. Query & Page Performance

![Top Queries](images/AOC_Top_Queries.png)<img width="2080" height="1239" alt="AOC- Top Queries" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/9b444770-f7f2-4e70-8bcd-b505e847da70" />

**Queries.** GSC reports 38 queries, which together cover only 285 of the 914 impressions (the rest are anonymized). Of those 285, **271 (95%) are brand-name searches** and misspellings ("amna omar", "amna omer", "amna clothing", "amnamuslim"). The non-brand queries are one-impression long-tail searches; "beige chikankari kurta set", "where to buy this dress", "find this dress"; but they are the first signs of **product-intent discovery** and worth tracking monthly.

![Top Pages](images/AOC_Top_Pages.png)<img width="2080" height="1280" alt="AOC- Top Pages" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/41280bf9-64c0-4876-83fa-20f286b272c8" />

**Pages.** The homepage (58 clicks) and `/collection/` (15 clicks) carry nearly all the clicks. Pages indexed grew to 12, including new product pages. `/product/serene-frost/` earned 2 clicks from 16 impressions, while `/product/beige-pearl/` has 82 impressions and zero clicks; the clearest page-level opportunity. Product-snippet appearances total 34 impressions with no clicks yet.

## 6. Google's 2026 Update Calendar (context for the data)

The campaign window overlaps an unusually busy year for Google Search, per Google's Search Status Dashboard and industry trackers:

| Date | Update |
|---|---|
| Mar 27 | March 2026 core update |
| May 21 | May 2026 core update (finished Jun 2) |
| Jun 24 / Aug 18 | Short spam updates |
| Sep 24 – Oct 8 | September 2026 spam update (about two weeks, the longest of the year) |
| Nov 10 | November 2026 core update; rolling out, may take two weeks or longer |

How to read it: the August position low sits between a spam update and the September one, and September's recovery came as that update started rolling. Correlation only; a site this small cannot isolate causes. For November, only 2 of the 11 days in this export fall inside the new core update, so **the November figures should be treated as preliminary**; re-pull GSC in early December for a clean read. Google's usual guidance applies: monitor during rollout, avoid reactive changes and judge results afterward.

## 7. Recommendations

| Priority | Action | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Add original content to product and collection pages (fabric, fit, sizing, styling, real photos) | Turns impression-only pages like Beige Pearl into clicking pages |
| 2 | Target non-brand, product-intent queries (kurta sets, chikankari, etc.) in on-page copy and a small style-guide/blog hub | Query data is ~95% brand today |
| 3 | Rewrite titles and meta descriptions for the homepage and `/collection/` | They already earn almost all clicks |
| 4 | Audit desktop performance (speed, layout, ranking gap) | Desktop position averages 12.9 vs 5.6 on mobile |
| 5 | Validate product schema and monitor the Product snippets report | 34 snippet impressions, no clicks yet |
| 6 | Test UK / US / Gulf landing content (shipping, sizing, currency) | Small but strong CTR in these markets |
| 7 | Re-export GSC in early December and compare against this baseline | Gets a clean post-core-update read |

## Methodology

- Source: one GSC Performance export (Chart, Queries, Pages, Countries, Devices, Search appearance tabs), Web search type, March 2 – November 11, 2026. A second export from March 27 was used only as an early baseline (it contains no traffic yet).
- Daily rows were summed to monthly totals. Average position is **weighted by daily impressions**. CTR = clicks ÷ impressions.
- Query, page, country and device tables are used exactly as exported; GSC omits anonymized queries from the query table.
- Summary data is in [`data/gsc_summary_mar-nov-2026.json`](data/gsc_summary_mar-nov-2026.json); charts were built with Python (matplotlib).
