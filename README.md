## 1. Clicks Vs. Impressions Trend

![Clicks vs Impressions](images/aoc_clicks_impressions_trend.png)<img width="1979" height="1110" alt="aoc_clicks_impressions_trend" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/d56411bc-b7e7-4c08-b3eb-b9ec11ab8aaf" />

**What happened:** Impressions climbed almost every month; from 6 in March to a peak of 93 in June; as Google crawled and indexed more of the site's pages (homepage, collection page, product pages, and policy pages). Clicks, however, peaked early in April (13 clicks) and then declined even as impressions kept rising.

**Why this matters:** A rising impressions line with a falling clicks line is a classic **CTR erosion pattern**. The site is being *shown* more often but *chosen* less often. That's a snippet/relevance/competition problem, not an indexation problem; and it is exactly where the next chart picks up the story.

---

## 2. CTR Vs. Average Position

![CTR vs Position](images/aoc_ctr_and_position_trend.png)<img width="1672" height="941" alt="aoc_ctr_and_ position_trend" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/cf8e7b66-03cf-4da7-b6ed-70d07eb3ea72" />

**What happened:** CTR fell sharply; from 35.1% in April to 5.6% by July; while the average ranking position also drifted from 4.3 down to 8.3 over the same window. The two metrics moved together: as rankings slipped further down page one, fewer of the growing impressions converted into clicks.

**Diagnostic read:** This is not a metadata problem alone. A position drop of roughly four spots on branded terms for a five-month-old site, occurring in the same window as two confirmed Google broad core updates in 2026 (see Section 6), points to a **relevance/authority gap**; the site does not yet have enough topical depth, backlinks, or on-page signal strength to hold position once broader competitor sets get re-evaluated.

---

## 3. Device Breakdown

![Device Breakdown](images/aoc_device_breakdown.png)<img width="2076" height="1267" alt="aoc_device_breakdown" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8754e354-bdc2-4b3e-bc22-adf5955d0858" />

- **Mobile drove 86% of all clicks** and 63% of impressions, at a much stronger CTR (17.0%) than desktop (4.9%).
- Desktop generated more impressions than expected but converted poorly; a common symptom of slower load times, layout shift, or a less polished above-the-fold experience on larger viewports.

**Action implication:** Since Google's ranking systems evaluate the mobile version of a site by default (mobile-first indexing) and Core Web Vitals; particularly Interaction to Next Paint (INP), which replaced First Input Delay in 2024; mobile performance should stay the primary optimization lens. Desktop's impression-heavy/click-light pattern is worth a dedicated UX audit.

## 4. Geographic Reach

![Top Countries](images/aoc_top_countries.png)<img width="1979" height="1199" alt="aoc_top_countries" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/a6754fab-43e1-4c91-a4ba-5c19009da06e" />

Pakistan leads on volume (117 impressions, 13 clicks), which is expected for a Pakistan-based store, but the **United Kingdom converts far better on far fewer impressions**; a 40.9% CTR against just 22 impressions. The United States and Canada also show healthy engagement. This is an early signal of an underserved diaspora/export audience that a dedicated shipping, currency, or landing-page strategy could capture before it shows up in the numbers organically.

## 5. Query & Page Performance

![Top Queries](images/aoc_top_queries.png)<img width="2061" height="1240" alt="aoc_top_queries" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/04c403ab-f3ad-45f7-a6c5-ba2eedc7a310" />

**The single most important finding in this dataset:** every query driving impressions is a **branded query**; "amna omar", "amna omer", "amna umar", and close misspellings of the brand name. There is not one non-branded, product-category, or commercial-intent query (e.g. "abaya online Pakistan", "modest wear UK", "women's [product type]") appearing in the top queries at all.

![Top Pages](images/aoc_top_pages.png)<img width="1979" height="1199" alt="aoc_top_pages" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/fe7dff76-8d58-4bd3-8162-e3a2943c6d9c" />

At the page level, the homepage and `/collection/` page absorb almost all clicks, while product pages like `/product/beige-pearl/` and category pages like `/tops/` and `/size/s/` are being **indexed and shown (38, 3, and 15 impressions respectively) but generate zero clicks**; they are impression-only pages, meaning Google is surfacing them for queries that are not yet compelling enough to earn the click, or the product/category content itself is too thin to rank for anything beyond incidental long-tail matches.

**Translation:** The site currently has a brand-search moat and nothing else. That is normal for month one to five of a new store, but it is *the* growth lever for the next phase; see roadmap below.

## 6. What's Changing in Google Search (2026); and Why It's Relevant Here

This account's performance window overlaps directly with an unusually active stretch of Google algorithm activity, which adds useful context to the CTR/position decline in Section 2:

- **March 2026 Core Update** (rolled out late March-early April 2026): one of the more disruptive core updates in recent memory, with independent trackers reporting significant top-10 volatility. Broad core updates re-evaluate content quality, relevance, and trust holistically rather than fixing one narrow issue; which is exactly the kind of update that can quietly cost thin, brand-only sites a position or two.
- **May 2026 Core Update**: Google's own status dashboard described it as a routine recalibration aimed at surfacing more relevant, satisfying content; but it followed an unusually long gap after March, and it specifically appeared to further devalue templated, low-depth, and scaled content patterns that do not demonstrate first-hand product or brand expertise.
- **May 2026 spam policy update**: Google extended its spam policies to explicitly cover manipulation of AI-generated answers (AI Overviews and AI Mode), formalizing enforcement against tactics aimed at gaming AI-driven results; a signal that content built for genuine usefulness, not just keyword matching, now carries even more weight.
- **2026 Search Quality Rater Guidelines refresh**: reinforced Google's E-E-A-T framework (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust) and continues to push scaled, generic, AI-assisted content further down in favor of pages that show real, first-hand product and brand experience; directly relevant to an e-commerce brand's product and collection pages.

**Why this matters for the roadmap:** none of this is a penalty; Amna Omar Clothing is simply operating in the same environment as every other site right now. But it does mean the fastest path back to CTR and position growth is not more pages, it is **deeper, more original, more experience-driven pages** (real product photography context, genuine size/fit guidance, authentic brand story) rather than templated e-commerce boilerplate.

## 7. Recommendations & Next-Phase Roadmap

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build out product & category page content (fabric, fit, styling guidance) beyond templated descriptions | Converts impression-only pages into ranking, clicking pages; aligns with 2026 E-E-A-T emphasis |
| 2 | Target non-branded, commercial-intent keywords in on-page copy and new blog/style-guide content | Currently 100% brand-query dependent; this is the single biggest visibility ceiling |
| 3 | Improve title tags & meta descriptions on the homepage and `/collection/` | These already earn clicks; sharpening the snippet compounds an existing win |
| 4 | Run a Core Web Vitals / INP audit on desktop | Desktop earns impressions but underperforms on CTR relative to mobile |
| 5 | Build a small set of quality backlinks / brand mentions (fashion directories, guest features, UGC) | Strengthens authority signals core updates are increasingly weighting |
| 6 | Consider a dedicated UK/US landing experience (shipping, currency) | UK shows 40.9% CTR on tiny volume; a clear, underexploited demand signal |
| 7 | Re-pull GSC data monthly and track branded vs. non-branded query split | The clearest health metric for this account going forward |

## Methodology

- Data pulled directly from two Google Search Console exports (`AOC- GSC Page Indexing` reports, dated March 27, 2026 and August 2, 2026), the second covering the full "Last 6 months" window used for this analysis.
- Daily Performance data (Clicks, Impressions, CTR, Position) aggregated to monthly totals; average position weighted by daily impressions rather than a simple mean, to avoid low-impression days skewing the trend.
- Query, Page, Country, and Device tables reflect the same 6-month filtered window, exported directly from GSC without modification.
- Charts built with Python (pandas + matplotlib) for full reproducibility; see [`/scripts`](scripts/) if included, or regenerate from the source `.xlsx` files.
